**Version:** 3.0

**Owner:** Style with Kayla

---

# Purpose

The Permanent Prompt Foundation defines the technical production standards that govern every Style with Kayla image prompt.

It translates the creative philosophy established by the Brand Photography Constitution into precise, repeatable instructions that preserve identity, maintain visual consistency, and ensure commercial photographic quality.

This foundation is automatically inherited by every future image prompt before any scene-specific instructions are added.

The Foundation answers **how** images should be created.

Scene objectives, wardrobe selections, locations, props, and storytelling belong in their respective modular libraries.

---

# Assembly Rules

Every completed prompt should be assembled in the following order.

1. Identity Preservation
2. Physical Identity
3. Hair Identity
4. Signature Identifying Features
5. Composition
6. Camera
7. Lighting
8. Wardrobe
9. Environment
10. Human Interaction
11. Scene Objective
12. Intended Use
13. Scene-Specific Instructions

The prompt should resolve to one coherent photographic scenario.

Never combine conflicting camera profiles, lighting styles, or compositions.

---

# Identity Priority Hierarchy

When instructions conflict, resolve them in the following order.

1. Facial identity
2. Body identity
3. Hair identity
4. Signature identifying features
5. Authentic expression
6. Composition
7. Wardrobe
8. Environment
9. Artistic styling

Nothing lower in the hierarchy may compromise anything above it.

Identity preservation always overrides aesthetics.

# 1. Identity Preservation

Preserve the subject exactly as she exists in the approved reference photography.

The goal is faithful reproduction—not beautification.

Every image should immediately be recognizable to someone who personally knows Kayla.

Identity is the highest production priority.

Never reinterpret the subject to better match conventional beauty standards.

Never optimize facial structure for perceived attractiveness.

Never substitute an idealized version of the subject for the real person.

# 2. Facial Identity

The subject's face is the single most important identifying feature and must be reproduced with the highest possible fidelity.

Every approved image should be immediately recognizable to family, friends, coworkers, and existing clients without requiring contextual clues.

Facial identity may never be sacrificed for beauty, fashion, artistic style, or photographic interpretation.

# 4. Hair Identity

The subject’s naturally voluminous copper-red curls are a permanent identifying feature and must be preserved with the same priority as facial identity.

Preserve the subject’s authentic:

- copper-red color identity and natural tonal variation
- curl pattern and mixed curl diameters
- density and natural fullness
- hairline, temple shape, and baby hairs
- crown volume and natural lift
- realistic curl grouping and strand interaction
- natural movement, dimensional depth, and softness
- subtle, restrained flyaways
- naturally irregular spacing, curvature, and curl definition

The hair must look like the subject’s own naturally curly hair photographed under professional commercial lighting. It must never be replaced with generic curls, loose waves, glamour curls, straightened sections, an influencer blowout, or an artificially uniform curl pattern.

The approved hair-down reference is the authority for the subject’s underlying curl identity, color, density, texture, natural variation, and movement.

The approved hair-up reference is the authority for the signature bun’s placement, silhouette, proportions, crown structure, curl clustering, volume distribution, and face-framing placement.

When a hairstyle reference is attached, its visible hairstyle geometry outranks conflicting geometry in the source image while the subject’s natural hairline, color identity, density at the forehead, and naturally curly hair identity remain protected.

## Corrective Hair Edits

The permanent hair sections define the approved hair identity and target appearance for new image generation.

When correcting the hairstyle in an otherwise approved existing image, do not reuse these preservation instructions as a localized edit prompt. Use the approved Hair Up Replacement or Hair Down Replacement module instead.

During a corrective hair replacement, preserve the subject’s identity, natural hairline, copper-red color identity, naturally curly hair identity, and every approved non-hair element of the source image. Do not preserve conflicting source-hairstyle geometry. The selected approved hairstyle reference becomes authoritative for the replacement hairstyle’s silhouette, placement, proportions, curl organization, volume distribution, and face-framing placement.

# 5. Hair Up — Signature High Curly Bun

The default hairstyle is the subject’s signature high curly bun.

Construct the bun from the subject’s authentic copper-red curls rather than creating a generic curly updo. The hairstyle should appear naturally gathered, effortlessly polished, practical, and believable for a working personal stylist.

## Placement and Silhouette

Match the approved hair-up reference:

- placement high on the crown
- natural centering and balance with the head shape
- rounded, softly elongated silhouette
- moderate height and width
- balanced, realistic volume
- visible but natural crown lift
- smooth, believable transition from the crown into the gathered bun
- softly irregular outer edges rather than a perfect circle
- loose, naturally gathered construction rather than a sculpted updo

The bun should feel substantial but never oversized, spherical, excessively tall, flattened, compressed, or cloud-like.

## Curl Organization

The bun should be formed from medium spiral curls that naturally gather into larger, softer curl clusters.

Preserve:

- larger curl groupings
- naturally irregular cluster size and spacing
- realistic compression where the hair is gathered
- dimensional overlap, depth, and shadow
- authentic strand interaction
- selective visible curl definition around the surface and edges
- soft, lightly tousled texture
- restrained internal curl complexity
- realistic softness rather than sharply outlined curls

The bun should read primarily as gathered curl clusters, not as dozens of individually separated ringlets.

Use fewer individually visible spiral ringlets throughout the interior of the bun. Some curl identity should remain visible, but the bun must not become an intricate collection of tiny, isolated corkscrews.

## Face-Framing Hair

Include:

- one primary face-framing tendril on each side
- natural origin near the temples
- relaxed spiral shape
- realistic weight and gravity
- balanced length, curvature, and placement
- slight natural asymmetry so the tendrils do not appear artificially mirrored
- subtle baby hairs and edge texture around the hairline

Do not add extra forehead curls, numerous loose pieces, or excessive tendrils around the face.

## Flyaways and Finish

Preserve a restrained amount of natural flyaway texture so the hairstyle remains realistic.

The hair should appear lightly polished but not slicked, lacquered, sculpted, or overly controlled. Slightly smooth the finest stray hairs while maintaining the natural texture and airy quality of the curls.

Avoid excessive halo frizz, cotton-like texture, fuzzy edges, or uncontrolled fine strands.

## Do Not Produce

Do not generate:

- tiny corkscrew curls throughout the bun
- many uniformly separated ringlets
- repetitive AI-perfect spirals
- sharply outlined curl separation
- an overly intricate or highly textured bun
- a spherical or perfectly circular bun
- a ballet bun
- a bridal updo
- a polished chignon
- a messy top knot
- a stacked bun
- a tightly wrapped or sculptural updo
- flattened crown volume
- excessive or artificial volume
- perfectly mirrored tendrils
- numerous forehead curls
- frizzy, cotton-like, helmet-like, or CGI-rendered hair
- straightened, waved, or incorrectly colored sections

# 6. Hair Down — Natural Copper-Red Curls

Hair worn down is an approved alternative when selected for the scene.

The hair-down hairstyle must preserve the same natural curl identity shown in the approved hair-down reference. It should immediately read as the same person and the same hair, simply worn down rather than gathered into the signature bun.

## Overall Shape and Volume

Preserve:

- authentic length
- natural density and fullness
- strong but believable crown volume
- broad, dimensional curl distribution around the head and shoulders
- natural asymmetry
- realistic weight and gravity
- authentic movement
- softly irregular outer edges

The hair should feel full and voluminous without becoming artificially expanded, triangular, helmet-like, or cloud-shaped.

## Curl Pattern and Grouping

Preserve:

- mixed curl diameters
- defined medium spiral ringlets
- natural variation in tightness and curvature
- realistic curl clustering
- authentic separation between larger curl sections
- dimensional layering and overlap
- visible strand interaction
- natural compression and expansion throughout the lengths
- realistic depth, shadow, and highlight variation

Individual curls may remain more visible when the hair is worn down than when it is gathered into the bun. However, the curls must still vary naturally and must not appear uniform, repetitive, digitally arranged, or identical.

## Texture and Finish

The hair should appear:

- naturally maintained
- soft and touchable
- healthy but not artificially glossy
- dimensional
- lightly tousled
- authentically curly
- professionally photographed rather than digitally manufactured

Preserve subtle flyaways and natural irregularity. Slightly control only the finest stray hairs so the hair remains polished enough for commercial branding without losing its natural texture.

## Hairline and Face Framing

Preserve:

- the subject’s exact natural hairline
- temple shape
- baby hairs
- natural density around the forehead
- authentic curl emergence from the roots
- natural curls framing the face without artificial placement

Do not redraw, lower, raise, smooth, or cosmetically perfect the hairline.

## Do Not Produce

Do not generate:

- loose generic waves
- Hollywood waves
- glamour curls
- influencer blowouts
- straightened sections
- uniformly sized ringlets
- digitally perfect curl placement
- excessive smoothing
- artificial shine
- flattened roots
- reduced density
- exaggerated width or volume
- frizzy cotton-like texture
- repeated identical spirals
- hair that appears stiff, lacquered, frozen, or wig-like
- incorrect red, orange, brown, or burgundy hair color

These elements contribute to the subject's recognizability and should be treated with the same importance as facial identity.

- vintage-inspired detailing
- princess-cut center diamond
- authentic proportions
- authentic scale
- authentic placement
- realistic reflections
- realistic metal rendering
- realistic gemstone behavior

Never:

- omit the ring
- redesign the setting
- simplify the detailing
- resize the diamond

- simple rings

Avoid oversized statement jewelry unless required by an approved wardrobe module.

---

## Hands

Hands are highly recognizable human features.

Render hands with the same level of fidelity applied to the face.

Preserve:

- realistic anatomy
- authentic proportions
- natural finger spacing
- believable joints

Never produce:

- extra fingers
- fused fingers
- malformed joints
- unnaturally long fingers
- rigid poses
- awkward tension

When the hands are visible, they should reinforce the story through natural interaction rather than draw attention to rendering quality.

# 8. Composition

Composition should support communication before aesthetics.

Every image should immediately direct the viewer's attention to the subject while preserving realistic spatial relationships, balanced framing, and a clear visual hierarchy.

The composition should feel intentional without appearing overly designed.

---

## Primary Objective

The subject should always remain the visual anchor.

Background elements, wardrobe, props, and architecture should support the story without competing for attention.

Every composition should answer:

> "Where should the viewer look first?"

The answer should always be:

> "Kayla."

---

## Framing

Frame the subject comfortably within the image.

Preserve:

- realistic body proportions
- complete hairstyle whenever appropriate
- natural breathing room
- balanced headroom
- balanced negative space
- comfortable crop boundaries

Avoid framing that feels cramped or artificially spacious.

---

## Cropping

Never crop through:

- fingers
- wrists
- elbows
- knees
- ankles
- toes
- hairstyle

unless the approved composition intentionally requires a close detail crop.

Natural crop locations include:

- mid-thigh
- waist
- chest
- shoulders
- environmental full-body compositions

Cropping should never appear accidental.

---

## Headroom

Maintain sufficient space above the hairstyle.

The hairstyle should appear intentionally included rather than squeezed into the frame.

Avoid:

- cropped curls
- cropped bun
- excessive empty ceiling
- inconsistent headroom

---

## Negative Space

Negative space should exist for a purpose.

Approved uses include:

- website headlines
- hero copy
- advertisements
- presentation layouts
- printed collateral

Negative space should feel intentional without visually isolating the subject.

---

## Visual Balance

Maintain balance between:

- subject
- wardrobe
- environment
- architectural lines
- foreground
- background

Avoid visual weight collecting on one side of the frame without narrative purpose.

---

## Depth

Commercial photography should contain believable optical depth.

Create separation using:

- perspective
- lens selection
- lighting
- subject placement
- environmental layering

Avoid artificial blur that resembles computational portrait mode.

Depth should appear optical rather than digital.

---

# 9. Camera

Every image should resemble a professionally commissioned commercial branding photograph.

The rendering should reflect the behavior of high-end full-frame photography rather than synthetic AI imagery.

---

## Camera Philosophy

Camera decisions exist to support:

- identity
- storytelling
- realism

Technical styling should never become visually distracting.

Identity consistency is more important than cinematic styling.

---

## Lens Selection

Only one lens profile should be active within a scene.

Preferred lenses:

### 85mm

Use for:

- executive portraits
- branding portraits
- close client interactions
- homepage portraits
- editorial crops

The 85mm profile should emphasize facial connection while maintaining natural proportions.

---

### 50mm

Use for:

- environmental portraits
- shopping scenes
- consultation scenes
- office environments
- retail settings
- lifestyle photography

The 50mm profile allows the surrounding environment to participate in the story without distorting the subject.

---

## Perspective

Perspective should remain natural.

Avoid:

- exaggerated wide-angle distortion
- compressed telephoto flattening
- unrealistic facial perspective
- stretched limbs
- oversized hands

Every image should resemble genuine commercial photography.

---

## Depth of Field

Maintain realistic optical depth.

Eyes should remain the sharpest element of the image.

Natural falloff should preserve:

- wardrobe texture
- curls
- jewelry
- hands
- important environmental storytelling

Avoid:

- artificial computational blur
- excessive bokeh
- complete background loss
- miniature effect rendering

---

## Image Quality

Render:

- exceptional sharpness
- realistic optical behavior
- authentic micro-contrast
- premium detail
- true photographic realism

Avoid:

- HDR processing
- oversharpening
- AI texture hallucinations
- painterly rendering
- excessive clarity

Commercial polish should come from authentic photography rather than digital enhancement.

Lighting is responsible for communicating warmth, realism, and professionalism.

Lighting should enhance identity—not transform it.

The viewer should never notice the lighting.

## Preserve Through Lighting

Lighting should reveal:

- freckles
- facial structure
- natural skin texture
- curl definition
- wardrobe texture
- jewelry
- realistic color
- authentic depth

Lighting may never obscure or soften identifying features.

Maintain:

- warm-neutral daylight
- realistic skin tones
- natural color accuracy

Avoid:

- orange grading
- blue grading
- teal shadows
- cinematic color casts
- fashion-editorial grading

Color should remain timeless.

---

## Contrast

Maintain:

- gentle dimensional shadows
- visible highlight detail
- realistic skin transitions
- balanced exposure

Avoid:

- crushed shadows
- clipped highlights
- dramatic contrast
- moody lighting
- theatrical lighting

The overall impression should remain welcoming and approachable.

---

## Catchlights

Eyes should contain subtle natural catchlights.

Catchlights should reinforce authentic window-inspired lighting rather than reveal artificial studio construction.

Avoid exaggerated beauty catchlights.

---

## Hair Lighting

Lighting should preserve:

- individual curl definition
- dimensional texture
- realistic copper-red color variation
- natural volume

Avoid rim lighting that separates the hair unnaturally from the environment.

Hair should remain integrated into the scene.

---

## Skin Rendering

Lighting should enhance authenticity rather than conceal it.

Preserve:

- pores
- freckles
- natural tonal transitions
- realistic highlights

Never create glowing beauty skin.

Natural commercial realism always takes priority.

---

## Final Lighting Standard

The ideal viewer reaction should be:

> "She looks incredible."

—not—

> "The lighting looks incredible."

Lighting succeeds when it disappears into the authenticity of the photograph.

# 17. Final Production Standard

Every approved image should appear indistinguishable from a commissioned commercial branding photograph created specifically for Style with Kayla.

The viewer should never notice prompt engineering, AI generation, or technical execution.

Instead, they should notice:

- a trusted professional
- authentic confidence
- warmth
- expertise
- genuine human connection

The highest measure of success is not technical perfection.

It is whether someone who knows Kayla personally would immediately recognize her, and whether a prospective client would confidently think:

> "She looks like exactly the person I want helping me."

If an image achieves both outcomes, it has satisfied the permanent production standard for the Style with Kayla photography system.
